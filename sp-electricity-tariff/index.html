How the tariff is set

SP Group revises the tariff every quarter, usually on the last working day before the new quarter starts. Most of it is the energy cost, which follows natural-gas prices over roughly the first two and a half months of the previous quarter. The rest — network costs, market support and the market administration fee — barely moves.

So the January–March 2027 tariff will be announced around 31 December 2026, and it will track gas prices from October to mid-December. This page is updated when it lands.

Questions

Frequently asked

What is the SP electricity tariff for October to December 2026?

28.59¢/kWh before GST, or 31.16¢/kWh including 9% GST, for 1 October to 31 December 2026. That is 10.4% lower than the 31.91¢ (34.78¢ incl. GST) charged from July to September 2026.

When is the next SP tariff announcement?

SP Group announces each quarter’s tariff around the last working day of the previous quarter, so the January–March 2027 tariff is expected around 31 December 2026.

Why did the electricity tariff jump in July 2026?

The energy cost component follows natural-gas prices in the first two and a half months of the previous quarter. Gas prices were very high from April to mid-June 2026 because of the Middle East conflict, so the household tariff rose 17% for July–September.

Is a fixed-price plan cheaper than SP’s tariff?

In each of the last five quarters (Oct 2025 to Dec 2026), yes: the lowest SP tariff in that period was 29.11¢ including GST, above the 28.90¢ that PacificLight’s 3-year plan charged on 24 Oct 2026. A fixed plan locks the rate for the whole contract, so it can lose if SP’s tariff falls far enough.

Do I keep U-Save rebates if I switch retailer?

Yes. U-Save rebates still apply when you buy electricity from a retailer under the Open Electricity Market.
