What the free insurance actually is

Twelve months of complimentary cover, and you choose one of three: Home, Travel, or Personal Accident. It's arranged through Professional Investment Advisory Services and underwritten by Singlife. Cover starts 14 days after your application. It's open to new customers and renewing ones, on any plan of 12 months or longer — so unlike the referral credit, you don't need a 24-month contract to get it.

One honest caveat: Tuas doesn't publish the sums assured or coverage limits for any of the three options — the terms tell you to contact the adviser for details. So treat it as a real perk of unknown size, not a quantified $X of value. Worth asking what the Home cover actually pays out before you count it toward your decision.

The rebate is a bill credit, and that has a catch

Tuas's National Day campaign let you take the rebate as a grocery e-voucher instead of a bill credit. The ONLINE campaign that replaced it on 1 September does not — it is a bill credit only. That matters because a bill credit only pays off if you stay: leave early and Tuas claws the whole thing back, on top of the $200 termination charge. The free insurance, not the rebate, is now the thing Tuas has that the others don't.

Tuas's referral terms limit the reward to PowerFIX 24 and PowerFIX 36, signed up online. PowerFIX 12, PowerDOT 6, and anything signed at a roadshow do not qualify — the field is there but the credit never comes.

The tiered structure is unusual — most retailers pay a flat referral. With Tuas, if the person you refer goes on to refer someone else, you keep earning down to three levels, up to $35 per chain, with no cap on how many people you refer.

A discrepancy worth knowing about: Tuas's Refer a Friend page states that "you and your friend" each get $20, and customer reports on Seedly are consistent with the new customer receiving it. But the programme's own terms document only spells out the referrer's side of the payment. Your S$20 is very likely, and it's what Tuas advertises — it just isn't written into the contract terms. Nothing to lose by entering the code either way.

Questions

Frequently asked

Does this Tuas Power referral code still work?

Yes, as at 13 October 2026. Tuas's Refer a Friend programme runs from 1 January to 31 December 2026, and referral codes are tied to a customer account rather than a campaign, so they don't expire mid-year. A code that appears not to work almost always means the selected plan isn't eligible.

How much is the Tuas Power referral reward?

S$20 off the bill. Tuas's Refer a Friend page says both the new customer and the referrer get S$20; the referrer's side is also tiered, earning S$10 at the second level and S$5 at the third, up to S$35 per referral chain. The new customer's S$20 is credited on the second or third bill after the transfer completes.

Which Tuas Power plans are eligible for the referral code?

Only PowerFIX 24 and PowerFIX 36, signed up online. PowerFIX 12, PowerFIX 25 G+, PowerDOT 6, and any sign-up done at a roadshow do not qualify.

Can the referral code be stacked with the sign-up rebate?

Tuas's current ONLINE campaign rebate ($100 on PowerFIX 24, $160 on PowerFIX 36) and the S$20 referral credit are separate programmes, and customer reports describe receiving both — up to S$180 on PowerFIX 36. But Tuas’s own promotions page says the referral “bill rebate will be revised when combined with another promotion”, so the full S$20 on top of the campaign rebate is not guaranteed — treat S$180 as the advertised ceiling, not a certainty.

What is the free insurance that comes with Tuas Power plans?

Twelve months of complimentary cover, choosing one of Home, Travel or Personal Accident. It's arranged through Professional Investment Advisory Services and underwritten by Singlife, with cover starting 14 days after application. It's available to both new and renewing residential customers on any plan of 12 months or longer.

Tuas doesn't publish the sums assured or coverage limits, so ask the adviser what the cover actually pays before counting it toward your decision.

Is Tuas Power cheaper than PacificLight?

No — PacificLight is cheaper, as at 13 October 2026. The two repriced in opposite directions in October: PacificLight cut to 28.90¢ on 36 months and 29.00¢ on 24, while Tuas rose to 30.63¢ and 30.90¢. That is about 1.7¢/kWh, or roughly $242 over three years at average usage — more than Tuas's $160 rebate recovers. Tuas's remaining advantage is the 12 months of free insurance, which no rival offers. If you don't value that, PacificLight is the cheaper choice on both rate and advertised rebate.

Does Tuas Power still have a 6-month fixed plan?

No. PowerFIX 6 still appears on an outdated promotions page on Tuas's own site, but there's no current factsheet for it and it isn't on the live plans page. The only 6-month option is PowerDOT 6, which is a 10% discount off the regulated tariff — a floating rate, not a fixed one.

What happens if I terminate my Tuas Power contract early?

A $200 early termination charge applies on every PowerFIX and PowerDOT plan, and Tuas claws back the full value of any bill rebate already paid — including the referral credit. That's on top of losing the fixed rate. Worth weighing before taking the 36-month plan for its larger rebate.

Will my U-Save rebates still apply if I switch to Tuas Power?

Yes. Billing stays with SP Group after you switch retailer, so U-Save and GST Voucher rebates continue to offset your bill automatically with nothing for you to do. This is true of any Open Electricity Market retailer, not just Tuas.
